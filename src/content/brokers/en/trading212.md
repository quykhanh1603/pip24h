## Regulation and safety

Trading 212's help centre lists Trading 212 UK Ltd as FCA-authorised (reference 609146) and Trading 212 Markets Ltd as CySEC-licensed (398/21). The FCA register shows the UK firm, and the CySEC register shows licence 398/21 dated 1 March 2021. The help centre also lists Trading 212 EU GmbH (BaFin) and Trading 212 AU Pty Ltd (ASIC); we did not check those registers. Wikipedia reports that the company allowed UK retail customers to buy crypto exchange-traded notes between October 2025 and January 2026 before the FCA permission was in place.

## Trading conditions

Trading 212 describes itself as commission-free. For CFDs, costs come from variable spreads, overnight interest and a 0.5% FX fee on closed positions when currencies differ. The company's learning pages say CySEC rules cap retail leverage at 30:1 on major forex pairs. The minimum deposit varies by region and account type.

## Platforms

Clients trade through Trading 212's own web platform and mobile app, covering listed shares, ETFs, CFDs and forex.

## Who is it for?

It suits beginners and long-term investors who want a low-cost app, plus CFD traders happy with a proprietary platform. Those who need MetaTrader or advanced order tools may look elsewhere. CFDs carry a high risk of losing money.
