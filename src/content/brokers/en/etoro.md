## Regulation and safety

eToro's regulation page lists several group entities. eToro (Europe) Ltd holds CySEC licence 109/10, which we confirmed on the CySEC register. eToro (UK) Ltd is listed under FCA reference 583263; the FCA's own clone-firm warning names it as the genuine authorised firm. The same page also lists ASIC (AFSL 491139), MAS (CMS101824), ADGM FSRA (permission 220073) and US SEC/FINRA-registered entities, which we did not check on those registers. Which entity serves you depends on where you live, and so do the compensation scheme and protections.

## Trading conditions

eToro offers real stocks and ETFs, crypto and CFDs. The homepage mentions investing from as little as $10 and a $50 start, but the minimum deposit varies by region. CFD costs are made up of spreads and overnight fees; check the current fee schedule for your jurisdiction.

## Platforms

Trading takes place on eToro's own web platform and mobile apps. CopyTrader and Smart Portfolios let users copy other traders or follow themed portfolios, and a demo account is available.

## Who is it for?

eToro suits beginners and investors who want social and copy trading in one app. Traders who need MetaTrader or very tight raw spreads may prefer a dedicated forex broker. Trading CFDs and crypto carries a high risk of loss.
