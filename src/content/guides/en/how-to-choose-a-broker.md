Choosing a broker is one of the first real decisions a new trader makes, and it's worth more care than picking whichever site has the flashiest ad — here's what actually matters.

## Regulation first

Check which authority licenses the broker (such as the FCA, ASIC, or CySEC) and in which entity your account will actually be opened, since brokers often operate multiple regulated entities with different protections. Regulation affects how your funds are protected if something goes wrong.

## Real trading costs

Look past the headline spread — check commissions, overnight swap fees, and any inactivity or withdrawal fees. Two brokers advertising "spreads from 0.0 pips" can have very different total costs once commissions are included.

## Deposit and withdrawal experience

Minimum deposit requirements, available payment methods, and — importantly — actual withdrawal processing times matter more once you're trading live. Check community reviews specifically for withdrawal experiences, not just account opening.

## Platform and instruments

Make sure the broker supports the platform you want to trade on (MetaTrader, cTrader, or a proprietary app) and offers the specific instruments — forex pairs, crypto, indices — you're actually interested in.

## Key takeaway

A good broker for one trader isn't automatically the right one for another. Match your priorities — cost, regulation, platform, or specific instruments — against what each broker actually offers before opening an account.
