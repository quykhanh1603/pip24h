A demo account lets you practice trading with virtual funds on live market prices, while a live account uses real money — and while the price data is the same, the experience of trading each one is not.

## What demo accounts are good for

Demo accounts are useful for learning a platform's interface, testing whether a strategy's rules make sense in practice, and getting comfortable placing different order types without financial risk.

## What changes with real money

The biggest difference isn't technical — it's psychological. Fear and greed generally don't show up in a meaningful way on a demo account, since there's nothing real to lose. Many traders who perform well on demo find their decision-making changes once real money and real emotions are involved.

## Other practical differences

Live accounts can also involve execution details a demo doesn't fully replicate, such as slippage during fast markets or the psychological pull of watching a real balance move up and down.

## Key takeaway

A demo account is a genuinely useful tool for learning mechanics, but it can't fully prepare you for the emotional side of trading — that only develops through experience trading smaller, real positions.
